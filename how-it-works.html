How it works

A daily call that turns into clarity.

There's nothing to install and nothing to learn. Brigid does the work of a daily check-in — the way a devoted nurse or a nearby daughter would — and quietly translates each conversation into something your family and care team can act on.

Step one

The call feels like a friend, not a form.

Each morning at a time that suits your loved one, Brigid calls. The conversation is unhurried and human — how did you sleep, how's the pain today, did you manage breakfast, have you taken your pills. Five to eight minutes, in their own language.

  • Works on a landline, a cell phone, a tablet or a computer
  • No apps, no buttons, no reading required
  • Multilingual, and gentle with low-tech, low-literacy users
Brigid
Good morning, Eleanor! How are you feeling today compared to yesterday?
Oh, much better, dear. The pain's really come down — maybe a 2. I walked to the kitchen and made my own toast.

That's wonderful. And did you take everything yesterday — the morning and the evening pills?
Step two

A warm voice, a clinical ear.

Underneath the small talk, Brigid is quietly running validated geriatric screening tools — the same instruments clinicians trust — woven into ordinary conversation so no one feels examined.

Daily function

ADLs and IADLs — dressing, bathing, cooking, managing medications — track whether independence is holding or slipping.

Heart & breath

NYHA and mMRC scales gauge how far someone can walk, climb, or exert before symptoms appear — early markers of decline.

Mind & mood

FAST staging and conversational cues surface confusion, low mood, or cognitive change that families often can't see over the phone.

Validated instruments include NYHA · CCS · mMRC · ADLs · IADLs · FAST

Step three

Every call ends in a colour.

Brigid's AI grades each conversation in real time, so a family member glancing at their phone knows in one second whether today was fine — or whether to pick up the phone.

Green · Stable

All is well.

Answers are within healthy ranges and recovery is on track. A reassuring note lands in the dashboard; no action needed.

Amber · Follow-up

Worth a closer look.

A missed dose, a poor night, a dip in mood. Flagged early and gently, so a small thing has the chance to stay small.

Red · Urgent

Time to act.

A warning sign that shouldn't wait. Brigid escalates to family and the care team so the right person is reached quickly.
